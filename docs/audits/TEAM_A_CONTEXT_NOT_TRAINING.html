The diagnosis

Surface A is 15 of 17 strings in Barry's own voice. Surface B is zero — Barry appears only in the third person, as a thing you configure, train, and that then executes on your behalf. A user who completes onboarding and then opens the help page meets two different characters: a colleague who asked about their business, and a system with a training progress bar.

Both surfaces are internally coherent. Neither is wrong on its own. The problem is that nothing connects them, so the flags cluster almost entirely on Surface B — 9 of 11 conflicts.

One coherent Barry story

Your governing principle already contains the spine. Four beats, in this order, on both surfaces:

1 · YOU GIVE BARRY CONTEXT          Recon. Not training — you brief him.
       "Tell Barry about your business, your customers, and how you win."

2 · BARRY CONNECTS THE DOTS         The intelligence, in first person where he speaks.
       "Barry connects your conversations, activity and relationships."

3 · IDYNIFY SHOWS WHO MATTERS       Scout. The product surfaces; Barry doesn't "deliver".
       "Matches based on your ICP, every weekday morning."

4 · YOU DECIDE WHAT TO DO NEXT      The user acts. Always.
       "Save the ones worth your time. Barry tells you why they matter."

Two voice rules that fix the split without rewriting Surface A:

  1. Where Barry speaks, he says "I". Surface A already does this — the one break is the step-6 empty state ("Barry will help you"), which should be "I'll help you find companies."
  2. Where the product is described, IDYNIFY is the subject and Barry is named as the intelligence inside it — never as an agent that executes or a system you train. Surface B inverts this everywhere. "Recon teaches Barry" becomes "Recon is where you give Barry context." "Barry executes it in Scout" becomes "Scout shows you who matters."

Applying those two rules resolves 9 of the 11 flags without any new copy invention. The remaining two — "prospecting engine" and the step-2 progress list — are judgment calls I'd want you on. My recommendation: replace "prospecting engine" with "I'll show you who's worth your time", and leave the progress list alone.

04

RECON terminology — the recommendation

The technical fact that decides it

RECON content is retrieved and injected into prompts. Nothing is trained. There is no model update, no weight change, no fine-tune. barryContextStack.js:292 extracts RECON sections into a context object for Barry's chat; barryBulkPersonalize.js:238 injects a reconBlock labelled — in the code itself — "SENDER'S BUSINESS INTELLIGENCE"; barryGenerateContext, barryGenerateMissionSequence and barryGenerateSequenceStep each load it the same way.

So "training" is not merely off-positioning. It describes a mechanism the product does not have. Every other criterion follows from fixing that.

All three candidate vocabularies already exist somewhere in the codebase, which is a strong signal the product has reached for them before: ReconOverview already renders a "Barry's Context Confidence score" section, /recon/alignment-brief already ships an Alignment Brief, and BarryKnowledgeButton.jsx is already named for knowledge.

SystemVocabularyUser understands?Avoids training framing?Sales + relationships?Describes what's stored?
A · CONTEXT
Recommended		 Give Barry context · Barry's context · Context Coverage · What Barry Knows · Add context Yes "Give Barry context" is plain English and matches the mental model of briefing a colleague. Yes Context is something you provide, not something a system absorbs about you. Yes Fully neutral. Context about a donor base reads the same as context about a sales territory. Exact Prompt context is literally what it is. Already the word the codebase uses.
B · BRIEFING Brief Barry · Barry's brief · Briefing complete · What's in the brief Yes Fits RECON's own military metaphor — you run recon, then you brief. Yes A briefing is unambiguously one-directional. Mostly Slightly operational; reads more naturally for a sales team than a fundraiser. Close But collides with the existing Alignment Brief at /recon/alignment-brief, which is a different artifact. Overloading it would make two things share one word.
C · KNOWLEDGE Barry's knowledge · What Barry knows · Knowledge coverage Yes "What Barry knows" is the clearest phrase of the three — and is already the subtitle at BarryTraining:157. Partly "Barry's knowledge" edges toward the AI-brain framing — knowledge implies something retained and grown, which is the implication we are removing. Yes Neutral. Partly Nothing is retained between sessions beyond the stored document; "knowledge" oversells persistence.

Recommendation: A, with C's best phrase kept

Context is the canonical system — it is the only one of the three that is literally accurate about the mechanism, it is fully neutral across sales and relationship use cases, and it is already the word ReconOverview uses for its confidence score.

But keep "What Barry Knows" as the name of the status page, because it is the clearest user-facing phrase available and it already exists as that page's subtitle. The distinction that makes both work: context is the thing you give; what Barry knows is the readout of how much of it he has. One is the verb side, one is the noun side.

Reserve brief for the Alignment Brief. It already means something specific.

06

PLATFORM_IMPACTS — Scout's three options

After actions 12–14, Scout's entry in ReconOverview's PLATFORM_IMPACTS has zero effects. Hunter's and Barry's entries are untouched and still populated.

OptionWhat the user seesHonest assessment
A · Remove Scout
Recommended		 RECON's impact section lists Hunter and Barry. Scout is absent. The only option that is true today and needs no new product decision. Cost: Scout is where users spend their time, so removing it weakens RECON's pitch and may reduce completion. That is a real cost, and it is the accurate one — RECON genuinely does not affect what Scout surfaces. Also the lowest-maintenance: nothing to revisit if the integration never ships. Risk: a user may notice Scout disappearing and read it as a regression.
B · One verified effect Scout · "RECON completeness increases the size of your discovery queue" True — QUEUE_TARGET = min(50 + floor(reconConfidence × 0.5), 100). But read it from the user's side: it says we cap your results at 50 until you do homework. That is a gate described as a benefit, and it may land worse than saying nothing. It also makes an internal throttle a public promise, which constrains you from changing the numbers later. Only viable if the behavior is first reframed as a feature you actually want to sell — which is the separate review you flagged.
C · Retain, label planned Scout · three effects, each badged "Coming soon" Weakest of the three. It keeps advertising unbuilt capability — a milder form of the original problem, now with a disclaimer. Three "coming soon" badges on a single module reads as an admission the module does not do what it says. Roadmap labels also rot: nobody removes them, and in six months they read as abandoned rather than planned. It converts a copy defect into a permanent visible commitment.
Recommendation

Option A now. Option B later, and only if the queue-size behavior is first reframed as a benefit rather than a gate.

A is the only choice that is honest today without requiring you to decide anything else first. If Scout's absence from the list bothers you, the right fix is issue #561 — build the integration — not a copy patch. And if you do want Scout represented in the interim, B is defensible, but the sentence has to be written so it reads as "more RECON, more to review", never as "less RECON, fewer results."
